Automatic, assisted or hands-on: choosing an espresso machine

← All buying guides

2 minute read · Facts checked October 28, 2026

Decide how much of the work you want to do, then choose the machine. Every machine in our catalog falls into one of three ways of working. The right one depends less on budget than on how you like your mornings.

The three ways of working

  • Automatic: press a button. The machine grinds, doses, brews and, on most models, steams the milk. Best for households where several people make their own drinks.
  • Assisted: a built-in grinder and help with dosing or tamping, while you still handle the portafilter and steam the milk. A good middle ground if you want to learn.
  • Hands-on: you control the grind, the dose, the tamp and the milk. The most involved and the most rewarding. You also need a separate grinder.

Measure the counter first

Published sizes differ between versions, so check the exact model. The JURA E8 manual we used lists about 11 in wide, 17.6 in deep and 13.8 in high. The Dinamica Plus is about 9.3 in wide, 16.9 in deep and 13.7 in high. Of the machines with published sizes, the Bambino Plus is the most compact, at about 7.7 in wide. Leave room above for the water tank and bean hopper, and add a grinder's footprint for the hands-on machines.

Questions to answer before you choose

  1. How many people will make drinks, and do they all want milk drinks?
  2. Would you enjoy learning to dial in a shot, or would you rather not think about it?
  3. How much counter width and height is free under the cabinets?
  4. Is there room for a separate grinder if you choose a hands-on machine?
