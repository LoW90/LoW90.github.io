Indoor or outdoor: choosing where your sauna lives

← All buying guides

2 minute read · Prices checked October 20, 2026

Choose by the room you already have, not by the photograph. An outdoor cabin becomes a small garden building, with a pad, a power run and a short walk in winter. An indoor sauna fits into a room you already have, so the questions shift to ventilation, the floor and the electric panel.

When outdoor is the better choice

Outdoor saunas suit a level, private corner of the garden with room for a delivery route. They give you a view, a place to cool off in the open air, and a ritual that feels like stepping away from the house. In return, you take on a foundation, an outdoor electrical run designed by a licensed electrician, drainage, and local setback or permit rules.

When indoor is the better choice

Indoor saunas suit a lower level, a home gym or a large primary bath. They are a few steps from a shower, usable in any weather, and add no garden structure. Ventilation, a floor that tolerates heat and moisture, and a dedicated, correctly rated circuit still need checking.

Five questions to answer before you choose

  1. Where would a delivery truck stop, and can a pallet reach the spot?
  2. Is there a level, well-drained area, or a room with a floor that tolerates heat and moisture?
  3. How far is it from the main electric panel?
  4. How many people will usually use it together?
  5. Will you use it in winter, and where would you cool down afterwards?
