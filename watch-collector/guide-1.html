Choosing a watch box: capacity, case size and a lock

← All collector guides

1 minute read · Facts checked October 28, 2026

Buy for the collection you expect to have, not the one you have today. Most collectors outgrow their first box. Count your watches, add a few, then check that your largest case fits the cushions.

Three things to check

  • Capacity: the number of slots. A drawer adds room for straps and tools, not more watches.
  • Case width: makers state the largest case a slot takes. The WOLF Windsor lists 52 mm and the Rothwell 10-slot box lists 60 mm. Thick cases and closed bracelets may still be a tight fit, so confirm with the seller.
  • Security: a lock and key keeps a box closed, but it is not a rated safe. For insured pieces, ask your insurer what storage they expect.

For travel

A travel case protects two or three watches in a bag. The Rothwell 2 Watch Travel Case is the one we priced. Its cushion fits wrists down to about 6⅛ in, so check that a closed bracelet fits.
